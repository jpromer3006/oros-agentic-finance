# Reply to Tendü, Markus and Yilu

**Subject:** Re: Capstone and HBS case feedback, and a follow up on longitudinal validation

Hi Tendü, Markus and Yilu,

Thank you for this, and thank you for the semester. I want to say plainly that I will remember it for the rest of my life. The speakers, the case work and the way the three of you kept pushing us past the obvious answer changed how I think about building things, not just how I think about AI.

I have been sitting with the line about rigorous longitudinal validation, and I agree with it completely. I also went looking for evidence against myself this week, and I found something that makes your point better than I could have made it.

The capstone portfolio was frozen on July 6 and never touched. Not a share, not a rule. On July 29, presentation day, it was 2.9 percentage points ahead of SPY. As of today it is 3.5 points behind. That is a swing of more than six points, and a change of sign, in twenty one trading days, with nothing changing except the calendar. That is exactly why the forward record I showed you cannot support a performance claim, and I would rather say that clearly than defend the number.

What I do think the project earned is the right to be falsified properly, and I have started writing the protocol for that rather than more features. In short:

1. The testable claim is about the ranking, not the portfolio. Detecting a Sharpe ratio of 0.5 at conventional significance takes roughly fifteen years of forward record. Detecting an information coefficient of 0.04 takes roughly fifty four decision dates. So the primary endpoint becomes the cross sectional rank correlation between the composite score and forward market relative return, clustered by decision date, which is answerable in months instead of decades.
2. Three independent tracks. A retrospective walk forward backtest from 2010 through 2025, corrected for survivorship and priced with realistic transaction costs. The forward out of sample record that has been accruing since May 26, with the stopping rule declared in advance so I cannot stop on a good number. And component ablations.
3. The ablation is the part I am most curious about, and I think it is the part that matters outside finance. If the deterministic scorer carries the ranking and the agent layer carries only the explanation and the provenance, that is a real and useful finding about where language models do and do not add value inside a decision system. I would rather publish that honestly than claim an edge I have not earned.

I also want to own something about how the project grew. My instinct was to keep it to the first tab and validate that one thing properly. What happened instead is that everyone I showed it to wanted another capability, portfolio construction, then deep research, then automation, and the forward thinking in this class made all of it feel possible, so I built it. The finished system did not exist until July, which left almost no runway for validation. If I ran the semester again I would freeze the scope in month two and spend the rest of it proving one claim.

Three things I would value your guidance on, and I know your time is scarce, so please answer only what is easy:

**On direction.** Do you see this as academic or commercial? The validation study I described looks like a paper to me, possibly on the agent layer ablation rather than on the trading system. If any of you think that is worth pursuing, and would be willing to point me at the right venue or advise informally, I would be grateful. On the commercial side I already know my two hard blockers, market data licensing and investment adviser regulation, and I would rather know now if the honest answer is that the research path is the stronger one.

**On the career pivot.** I am moving out of twenty four years at the MTA into AI and ML engineering, targeting forward deployed and applied AI roles. My honest gap is that my builds are personal scale rather than enterprise production. If you have a view on how someone with deep domain and operational experience but a short engineering track record should position for these roles, or on which of the two Fordham adjacent paths is stronger, I would take that advice seriously.

**On anything I am not seeing.** You have watched a lot of these projects. If there is an obvious weakness in OROS that I am too close to notice, I would rather hear it now.

Thank you again, sincerely. This was the best year of learning I have had.

Best,
